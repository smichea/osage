# Purpose

Show that a native client joins the same world as a browser, and give rendering work a real scene to be measured on.

# Concepts

- **Pose:** a kart to draw, as it is this frame. Whoever computes the karts writes poses; the scene only draws them.
- **Two sources of poses:** the network replica when playing, a local simulation in the bench.
- **Content is optional:** with the files of the asset manifest, surfaces are real materials; without, plain colours. The game runs either way.

# Architecture

Each frame has two stages. In `Simulate`, one of two plugins writes the poses: `play` applies what the world sent and predicts the player's kart; `bench` steps a local race of AI drivers ([osage-kart-racecraft](/modules/kart-racecraft.md)). In `Draw`, the scene modules read the poses: `track` (road, markings, kerbs, mown verges, barriers, the land with its knoll and lake), `scenery` (trees, bushes, flowers, rocks, clouds, balloons, mountains) and `landmarks` (start gantry and grandstand, tyre stacks and chevrons, arches, windmill, jetty and boat, fair with a big wheel; by the track's scenery, D-061: a lighthouse and its keeper's house on a coast, where `track` also draws the sea to the horizon and greys the ground into cliffs; rows of houses in a town; denser woods in a forest), both built with [osage-procedural](/modules/osage-procedural.md), `karts` (model and motion: wheels that spin and steer; the driver's hands on the wheel, the trunk a mass on a spring about the waist — thrown out of a bend by speed times rate of turn, forward under braking, back under acceleration, swinging a little past and settling, rattled on kerbs and grass — the helmet carried with it and the neck keeping the eyes nearer level), `effects` (particles), `sound` (a voice per kart made by [osage-kart-sound](/modules/kart-sound.md) from its state — the content's recordings where there are some, decoded at the start, synthesis otherwise — placed where it is and heard from the camera; the crowd in the grandstand; a cargo feature, `sound`, on by default — the bench is built without it and needs no sound library), and the chase camera: it follows a little late, looks into the bend the kart turns into, banks a little with it, widens with speed, and shakes as a following car would — a hum on asphalt, more on kerbs, bumps on grass, a jolt when the kart stops harder than brakes can (a smooth function of time: the bench's frames stay the same). Three views — behind (the default), the driver's eyes, high above — cycled with C or a gamepad's Select; Q, E and X held look left, right and behind, as does a gamepad's right stick, the head turning smoothly. `paddock` draws what the world sends of its paddock objects ([osage-kart-items](/modules/kart-items.md)): crates of amber glass with a question mark on each face (a texture drawn at the start; texture coordinates made again so each side's mark stands upright), bobbing and turning, broken at once where the player's kart is predicted to touch one, in a burst of confetti; tyres rolling, cones tossed, oil spreading, drones flying to the leader with their rotors turning and heard, a burst where one lands. Every kart carries what it holds on its back, small, for all to see (a nitro bottle, a spare tyre lying down — standing, it hid the driver from the chase camera — three for a stack, a cone, an oil can, a rocket, a smoke canister, a drone, an air bag, a red pit-crew flag); what it holds behind sits a metre and a half behind it, on the road; a kart under a push's guard glows in a bubble; a bumper used sends a ring out from the kart; a kart pouring smoke leaves a thick cloud of its colour. A kart with rocket left shows two rockets behind the seat (`karts`), with fire and a trail of smoke; a boost, nitro included, flames and smokes from the exhausts at every tier (`effects`). `hud` shows a `RaceView`, a plain description of the race that `play` fills, the item in hand included (how many tyres of a stack, whether it is held behind); F, Ctrl or a gamepad's North button uses it, and holding it keeps a tyre, a stack, a cone or oil behind the kart until it is let go (D-052). Between races, the arrow keys or a pad's d-pad choose the driver (left, right) and the kart body (up, down) from the world's roster ([osage-kart-roster](/modules/kart-roster.md), D-055), named in the panel under the prompt, where the cup being raced and its standings are shown too, with what is raced next (T chooses it for the host, a vote for the others; [osage-kart-cup](/modules/kart-cup.md), D-057; a time trial among them, D-062: the lap counter reads TRIAL, the ghosts of the best runs — [osage-kart-ghost](/modules/kart-ghost.md) — are drawn seen through, in one pale material, without shadow, sound, smoke or sparks, and the board shows each with the time it drives to); the player's kart is predicted with its build, and built again in its colour when it changes. The player's profile ([osage-kart-profile](/modules/kart-profile.md), D-064, `osage/kart-profile.txt`) gives the world the driver, kart and paint raced with last, once a connection, keeps every change, counts the player's points when a cup ends, and P paints the kart with the next paint won; karts are drawn in `PAINTS`, the team colours and four special ones. When the world's track changes (a cup's next race, D-056) the scene is built again round it; `--bench --track ID|FILE` benches a track under its sky. A panel under the prompt shows the world's records ([osage-kart-records](/modules/kart-records.md)): the best five laps, races and time trials, the player's marked, new ones flagged NEW!. Skill shows (D-050): the drift gauge and the sparks take the colour of the mini-turbo level charged (blue, orange, violet); over the circuit's crest the road, kerbs, verges, barriers, fences and land rise and fall together, drawn in half-metre pieces (`track`, D-051), and things on the road and the chase camera with them; a kart follows the crest's slope and its path through the air, and a trick rolls it over once; air streams past a kart in another's wake, and a stalled engine coughs black smoke (`effects`); during the countdown the HUD says to throttle as the lights go out, and the GO! becomes GREAT START! or STALLED. Escape opens the settings (`settings`): the frame rate wanted, sound on or off, music on or off, where the music comes from, and whether the radio directory is asked and for which kind of music — up and down pick one, left and right change it, and N and M switch sound and music at any time; they are kept in `osage/kart-settings.txt`. `music` plays through [osage-music](/modules/osage-music.md) (D-059): auto (the host's shared playlist when the world announces one, `Race.music_port`, waiting five seconds to hear from the world; else the game's own), the game's five CC0 pieces (`kart.playlist`, levelled, files in the content), the player's folder `osage/kart-music/`, the host's, or radio — the stations of `osage/kart-radios.txt` (written with an example the first time) and, when chosen, the directory's for a kind of music; B skips to the next piece or station, Shift+B back, and what plays is shown at the bottom for six seconds. Music is quieter under the karts (a half for pieces, a third for stations, which master louder), and the sound setting does not mute it. `voice` joins the world's voice conference ([osage-voice](/modules/osage-voice.md), D-060) under the driver's name when the world announces a relay (`Race.voice_port`): the others are heard at once; the microphone opens only for push to talk (V held) or an open microphone, chosen in the settings, where each other voice can be muted (left and right pick, Space mutes); who talks, and whether the player is heard, show above the music line. `show` presents the race (D-063): on the grid a camera flies the circuit low over the road, the chase camera easing down behind the kart when the countdown starts; the last six seconds of every kart's pose are kept, and the player's finish is shown again at half speed — beside the kart, then by the line — with REPLAY where the lights are; both set the camera through `Viewpoint`, and a viewpoint from the command line turns them off. With `--autodrive`, the host starts the race at once, and `--screenshot-after` takes a list of times, so that a passage can be shot frame by frame.

With the asset manifest's content ([osage-content](/modules/osage-content.md)) the circuit is photographed instead of toy-like: the sky is a photograph ([a photographed sky](/techniques/panorama.md)), at noon or, with `--sky evening`, in the late afternoon (the bench's is noon unless asked, and its report names the sky), the road (rubbered along the racing line, with skid marks through the bends), verges, gravel traps, kerbs and walls get real surfaces, the meadows vary in clumps and patches and no ground repeats: the road's grain is untiled from T2, the grass and gravel's from T3, and the road shows its history — a paving seam, repaired patches, cracks, stains — at every tier ([ground that varies](/techniques/ground-variation.md), D-054), concrete has a grain (steel and paint stay smooth), and from T3 the roadside is dressed with models and the land ringed by woods of tree cards ([osage-kart-roadside](/modules/kart-roadside.md), on [osage-props](/modules/osage-props.md)) in place of the toys; at T2 the woods of tree cards come to 35 m from the track, with procedural bushes and no procedural tree; at T1, procedural trees only. The start is a club circuit's: a galvanised gantry with its start lights in a black housing, a grandstand of concrete tiers with plastic seats and sculpted spectators in everyday clothes ([osage-kart-crowd](/modules/kart-crowd.md)), under a sheet-metal roof, chain-link fences between the walls and the spectators along the straights near the start, people standing behind them in small groups, marshal posts at the sharpest bends (a numbered post, an extinguisher, a marshal in orange with a yellow flag), a pit building across the straight (light concrete, six numbered roller doors under a canopy, two of them open on dark garages with a kart parked before each, its driver waiting in it and a mechanic in the team's colour beside it, a sign band, race control above behind a band of dark glass, windows at the ends), and advertising boards above the walls where the circuit bends gently, printed with the karts' sponsors' marks, no real brand. The land itself is a component ([osage-kart-land](/modules/kart-land.md)).

# Interfaces

Command line: a world's address to play, `--bench` to measure, `--tier`, `--sky noon|evening`, `--without TECHNIQUE`, `--autodrive`, `--screenshot-after`, `--camera X,Y,Z,TX,TY,TZ` to hold the camera at a place (pictures of the landmarks; a bench run with it is not the sanctioned bench). `--fps N|off` the frame rate wanted for this game. `OSAGE_ASSETS` names the content directory, or `none`.

Races: on the grid (`PHASE_READY`) the host — the first human driver — starts the race with Enter or a gamepad's North (the others see whom they wait for); a player racing alone can end the race from the settings (Enter); both go to the world as `Step.call`, carried by a few messages. Settings: Escape (or a gamepad's Start) opens them; the frame rate wanted (off, 30 to 144; 60 by default) is kept in `osage/kart-settings.txt` under `%APPDATA%` or `~/.config`, and the engine [holds that rate](/techniques/frame-pacing.md). While they are open, the keys do not drive.

# Invariants

- The scene never reads the network, and the network code never draws.
- Everything in the scene is placed by repeatable arithmetic, never by a random number generator: the bench draws the same frames on every run.
- The player's kart is predicted with the world's own physics, so that prediction is exact.

# Reuse

The split between "who computes poses" and "who draws them" lets the same scene serve a game, a replay and a benchmark. The procedural pieces (ribbons along a centre line, land that stays flat near a track) are described as algorithms and do not depend on Bevy.

# Limits

Karts are a real kart's shape and size ([osage-kart-model](/modules/kart-model.md)), moulded but without decals beyond a few stickers; the grandstand's spectators are mannequins, and the lake's jetty and boat are still toy-like. Modelled content for them comes with workstreams R8 and R9. The interface is not shown in the bench.
